# Parties

Open **Parties**, optionally **Configure match**, then **Create party**. Creation enters the match immediately. Friends paste the complete `tankyard://party/IP:PORT#invite` link into **Join a party**. The leader hosts the regular authoritative server on their own computer; up to eight humans can join.

**Internet setup: Automatic** finds the leader's public IPv4 address over HTTPS and requests a temporary UDP forward from a compatible UPnP router. The invite uses that public address and the actual mapped port. The worker never sends your invite or signing keys to a lookup service. Setup runs in the background; the invite address stays empty until discovery finishes, so an unnoticed LAN address isn't copied as an internet invite.

Open the **Party** tank icons, then **Invites / IP**, to copy the invite or edit **Public IP:PORT**. The status explains whether automatic forwarding succeeded. If it didn't, forward the displayed UDP port to the displayed local address and allow Tank Yard through your firewall. A public-IP lookup or successful router mapping alone cannot verify remote reachability. Double NAT needs forwarding at each router; carrier-grade NAT generally requires a public address from the ISP or a suitable reachable VPN host. The game does not change your firewall or create permanent router mappings. Only its own leased mapping is renewed and removed when the party ends; a crash lets it expire.

For a manually configured router or LAN-only game, choose **Internet setup: Manual / LAN** before creating the party. This disables external address lookups and router discovery. Enter the desired IP:PORT explicitly. The preferred game port is 5000; if occupied, the host uses an available port and shows that actual port in the setup instructions. The invite parser understands IPv4 and bracketed IPv6; the built-in party host listens on IPv4.

Click the tank-icon roster at the side of the match or main menu to expand **Your party**. It lists every member, their current tank, the leader and menu/match status. **Mute / Unmute** controls that member's voice playback locally; it does not silence them for everyone. Muting follows their connection across respawns and map changes. **Party chat** accepts ordinary typing and clipboard paste. Enter while alive or Slash opens the chat panel during play. New messages appear as an unread count on the collapsed roster. The panel adapts to the window; its shortcut moves aside while tank upgrades are open.

**Pause → Return to menu** retains your party, chat and hosting connection. **Resume party** or **Enter match** takes you back. The online match keeps running while you browse menus. Starting another session requires leaving first. Use **Your party → Leave party** and confirm to disconnect deliberately. If the leader leaves or closes the game, their server closes for everyone. The invite lasts for that hosted session; any invited member can share it, so share it privately.

Choose any offline mode, map and bot difficulty when configuring the party. Team slots range from two to eight. Human teammates replace allied bots; departing members are replaced by bots. Larger parties grow the opposing team. Co-op wave enemies remain mode-owned. Free For All and Maze retain individual combat; Tag and Infection retain conversion rules. The leader can change mode, map and difficulty in **Server tools** without losing party connections.

The local leader receives the **Server Host** badge through the process-local hosting channel. An invite grants membership only. Entry requires a fresh signature bound to the connection, server and administration session key. The invite seed stays out of saved server history and is never sent as a password. Direct UDP parties retain the regular unsecure netcode transport; invite proofs do not authenticate a server or encrypt chat/voice against an on-path observer. Creator and central-owner credentials remain separate.
